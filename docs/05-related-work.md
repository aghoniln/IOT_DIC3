## II. RELATED WORK

The development of conversational, data-grounded dietary agents sits at the intersection of **AI Technologies** (NLP, LLM Orchestration, RAG, Computer Vision) and **Nutrition Domain Knowledge** (Metabolic Energy Models, Nutritional Databases, Portion Estimation, Clinical Safety).

---

### A. AI Technologies in Nutrition

#### 1. Language Understanding & Food Entity Extraction (Food NER)
Extracting food names, portion sizes, preparation methods, and meal times from unstructured natural text is foundational for dietary dialogue systems. Early systems utilized Conditional Random Fields (CRF) and statistical Spoken Language Understanding (SLU) to parse voice-based meal descriptions [14]. With Transformer architectures, BERT-based token classification models significantly elevated entity recognition accuracy. However, static NER models struggle with cultural linguistic context, implicit quantities (e.g., "half a bowl of soup"), and multi-ingredient composite dishes common in Asian cuisines. DietSense leverages structured JSON Schema extraction combined with few-shot prompting in LLMs to decompose and extract hierarchical meal entities directly from conversational Traditional Chinese.

#### 2. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) & Tool-Augmented Agents
Direct query-answering via general-purpose LLMs introduces significant hallucination risks in clinical and numerical nutrition scenarios. Recent clinical reviews caution against unverified numerical output and dietary advice generated by standalone LLMs [12]. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) bridges this gap by grounding language models in external knowledge vectors. Furthermore, modern agentic architectures introduce whitelisted Function Calling / Tool Use, shifting the LLM’s role from a calculation engine to a high-level cognitive planner. Systems like ChatDiet [15] explore LLM orchestration alongside personal metabolic models, demonstrating superior transparency. DietSense builds upon this paradigm by adopting a strict **"LLM orchestrates, deterministic tools compute"** separation, ensuring all reported calories originate from auditable APIs rather than probabilistic text generation.

#### 3. Image-Based & Multimodal Dietary Assessment
Multimodal food analysis (e.g., Food-101 [17], Nutrition5k [18]) utilizes Convolutional Neural Networks and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to recognize food classes and estimate volume from photographs. While convenient, visual estimation faces severe real-world challenges: occluded ingredients, hidden cooking fats/oils, soup broth composition, and depth-scaling errors. A 2025 study evaluating GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 1.5 Pro revealed an energy Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of approximately **35.8%**, along with substantial systematic underestimation of large portion sizes [11]. Consequently, DietSense prioritizes text- and dialogue-based inputs as the primary interface, treating image recognition as an optional, secondary modality.

### B. Nutrition Domain Knowledge & Methodological Grounding

#### 1. Energy Balance & Basal Metabolism Equations
Energy balance ($E_{\text{intake}} - E_{\text{expenditure}} = \Delta E_{\text{storage}}$) governs human body weight dynamics [5]. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is computed as Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) multiplied by a physical activity level factor (1.2–1.9). Among predictive formulas (Harris-Benedict, Katch-McArdle, Mifflin–St Jeor), the **Mifflin–St Jeor equation** [8] has been proven by clinical reviews (e.g., Frankenfield et al.) to predict resting energy expenditure within $\pm 10\%$ of indirect calorimetry in the largest percentage of both non-obese and obese individuals. DietSense implements deterministic Mifflin–St Jeor logic while explicitly presenting the equation's boundary conditions (e.g., unsuitability for pregnancy or elite athletic muscle mass).

#### 2. Macronutrient Composition & Dietary Reference Standards
Calories alone do not determine satiety, hormonal responses, or long-term health outcomes. According to the Institute of Medicine (IOM) Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Ranges (AMDR) [9], optimal energy distribution should fall within:
- Carbohydrates: 45%–65%
- Proteins: 10%–35%
- Fats: 20%–35%
Energy conversion is anchored in Atwater physiological fuel values (4 kcal/g for carbohydrates and proteins, 9 kcal/g for fats). DietSense assesses daily meals against AMDR baselines and Taiwan Daily Dietary Guidelines, alerting users when single-nutrient distributions skew dangerously.

#### 3. Portion Estimation Error & Uncertainty Quantification
Self-reported dietary intake contains systematic cognitive bias, with under-reporting rates documented between 18% and 54% [6]. In real-world dining, colloquial units such as "one bowl (一碗)", "one piece (一份)", or "half the soup (湯喝一半)" vary widely in physical weight. Rather than emitting a falsely confident point estimate (e.g., "652 kcal"), DietSense models uncertainty as bounded intervals $[E_{\text{low}}, E_{\text{high}}]$ derived from local container measurement tables (P10–P90 percentiles) and retrieval match scores.

#### 4. Safety Guardrails & Disordered-Eating Protection
Commercial dietary trackers frequently face criticism for enabling or exacerbating disordered eating behaviors (such as anorexia or orthorexia) by encouraging aggressive caloric restriction. The American College of Sports Medicine and clinical guidelines recommend a minimum intake threshold (typically 1,200 kcal/day for adult females and 1,500 kcal/day for adult males) to avoid severe micronutrient deficiency and metabolic slowdown. DietSense implements rule-based hard boundaries that intercept targets below 1,200 kcal/day, detect binge–restrict patterns, and immediately route vulnerable users to professional medical care.

#### 2. Research Gap Convergence
Existing research demonstrates fractured progress:
1. **Computer Vision Track:** Image recognition apps provide frictionless entry but suffer from high error rates (~36% MAPE) and fail to capture hidden preparation details.
2. **Commercial App Track:** Databases are comprehensive, but manual form-filling imposes high cognitive friction, resulting in steep attrition.
3. **LLM Benchmark Track:** Demonstrates impressive linguistic understanding of meal text, but standalone models hallucinate ungrounded numbers and offer inadequate clinical guardrails.
4. **Localization Gap:** Western databases (USDA) fundamentally lack reliable decomposition for Taiwanese night-market foods, boxed lunches (便當), and composite soups.

#### 3. How DietSense Bridges the Gap
DietSense directly reconciles these trade-offs by integrating:
$$\text{Natural Dialogue} + \text{Taiwanese Food Anchoring} + \text{Tool-Based Determinism} + \text{Uncertainty Propagation} + \text{Rule Safety Guard}$$
This transforms passive calorie logging into a reliable, active, and culturally aware domain agent.
